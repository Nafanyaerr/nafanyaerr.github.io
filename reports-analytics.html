Reports & Analytics

WeFix needed a way for managers to see how the business was doing and understand why performance changed.

Overview

Context

As WeFix grew, its platform collected more data on customers, jobs, payments, employees and branches. Some metrics lived in the platform. Others were spread across Google Analytics, spreadsheets and separate reports.

My role

I studied how employees used data, developed hypotheses, chose visualisations and designed the interfaces. I worked with engineers to test the reports against real data.

01. The challenge

To prepare a report, managers had to gather and compare figures from several sources by hand. That left less time to analyse results, support their teams and resolve day-to-day problems.

Employees could also interpret the same metric differently.

02. Research

How employees used reports

I reviewed internal reports, Google Analytics charts and the other tools employees used. I then spoke with employees across roles about which reports they opened, which figures they collected by hand and what they compared.

The interviews focused on five questions.

  • What questions did they use data to answer?
  • Which figures did they need at a glance?
  • Which metrics did they explore in detail?
  • Which tasks took the most time?
  • What information was missing?

I turned their answers into a list of business questions and reporting requirements for each role. The engineers then mapped the data available to answer them.

Competitive analysis

I compared analytics products and data visualisation tools. I looked at how they organised dashboards, prioritised metrics, compared periods, handled filters, linked totals to individual records and showed trends or unusual values.

I studied visualisation principles and chose formats for each question. I reviewed them with the front-end engineers early on to understand what their charting libraries supported and how much work each option would take. We kept the simpler option when extra detail added little insight.

Four hypotheses

  • Bringing key metrics together on one dashboard would help managers assess the business more quickly.
  • Showing each figure alongside its trend and a point of comparison would make it easier to interpret.
  • Keeping detailed analysis in separate reports would make the dashboard easier to scan.
  • Giving each role access to the reports it needed would help employees find relevant data and protect sensitive information.

Ideas and constraints

For each business question, I proposed several ways to present the data. A trend could appear as a number, a line chart or a comparison between periods. A ranking, a table or a set of KPIs could show how employees were performing.

The stakeholder, engineers and I weighed how easy each option was to understand, what data we had, how complex the calculations were and what we could build in the first release. We simplified or deferred ideas that depended on unreliable data or complex aggregation.

03. Design decisions

Separate the overview from detailed analysis

We brought current figures and summaries together on one dashboard so managers could assess the business at a glance. Dedicated reports covered jobs, employees, service areas, communications and payments.

We showed figures alongside trends and benchmarks. Managers could see how jobs were distributed over time and compare current performance with average values.

Keep the filters when opening individual jobs

Once managers spotted a change, they needed to investigate its cause. I designed filters for service area, date range and job status.

From a report, users could open Jobs with the same filters already applied. That took them straight from a summary figure to the records behind it and the actions they could take.

Tailor report access to each role

Employees in different roles needed different reports. We gave dispatchers access to the sections they used in their work. The navigation showed the available reports up front, so dispatchers could see where they could go before choosing a section.

04. Testing with real data

Charts could behave differently once they contained real data. I worked with engineers in a test environment to check the calculations, filters and date ranges. We tried different values, checked empty states and followed the path from a summary figure to its underlying records.

We used what we learned to refine the charts, the underlying data structure and the choice of metrics.

Results

Managers could assess performance more quickly. In one place, they could compare branches and employees, track KPIs and open the records behind a figure.
